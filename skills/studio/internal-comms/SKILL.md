# Internal communications

Identify the audience, purpose, channel and source facts from the request. Follow an existing template or house style when supplied; otherwise use a concise structure suited to the message. Clarify only missing information that changes the substance.

- **Status update:** what changed, evidence of progress, current blockers, next steps and decisions needed. Separate completed work from plans.
- **Announcement:** what changes, whom it affects, when it takes effect and what recipients should do.
- **Incident update:** confirmed impact, current status, actions underway and next update if known. Distinguish hypotheses from established causes; do not invent resolution times.
- **Handover:** current state, outstanding work, dependencies and named ownership only when supplied.
- **FAQ:** direct answers to actual reader questions, with relevant sources and explicit unknowns.

Preserve names, figures, commitments and dates accurately. Use the audience's language and locale; make time zones and ambiguous numeric dates explicit when they matter. Avoid country-specific business or regulatory assumptions.

Match detail to the intended audience and channel. Keep sensitive source detail only when needed for the requested recipients; do not widen distribution. Do not manufacture praise, metrics, approvals or commitments.

Provide the requested draft and identify material gaps. Drafting does not authorize sending or publishing.
