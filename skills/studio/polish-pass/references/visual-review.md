# Visual review

Read the artifact, brief and actual styles/tokens. Preserve the requested or established visual direction. For screenshots, limit findings to what is visible. Respect review-only requests; when fixes are requested, make bounded edits and retain working interactions.

## Hierarchy and rhythm

Identify what readers should notice or do first. Check whether position, size, weight, contrast and spacing support that priority, accounting for reading direction and viewing context. Supporting content should not compete with the primary task.

Compare related components for unintended differences in alignment, padding, density and typography. Reuse a coherent scale while allowing deliberate optical adjustments. Distinguish intentional whitespace and repetition from missing content or monotony. Check narrow widths and zoom when possible.

Fix the smallest coherent set of layout rules or tokens. Do not mandate a saturated primary action, fixed palette size, font family, spacing grid or decorative variation. Inspect the effect across affected components.

## Generic design choices

Look for choices that weaken this particular artifact: placeholder copy, irrelevant imagery, repetitive containers, inconsistent tokens, unsupported figures or interchangeable section structures. Explain the concrete problem before recommending a replacement.

Gradients, cards, serif type, system fonts, rounded corners and neutral palettes are valid choices, not evidence of AI authorship. Do not infer who created a design or replace it merely because it matches a trope list. Use the brief to judge whether the structure and imagery serve the subject.

Prefer useful content and hierarchy over switching to another preset. Correct unsupported claims or label sample content; do not introduce new facts as decoration. No prescribed colors, fonts, icon pack or branding.

Recheck the result against the brief and summarize consequential findings/changes. Distinguish confirmed defects from subjective choices.
