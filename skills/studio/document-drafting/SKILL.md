# Structured document drafting

Read supplied material, the existing draft and any required template. Identify the audience, purpose and decision/action the reader needs to take. Ask only for gaps that prevent a useful draft; otherwise proceed with stated assumptions. Do not force a workshop or repeated approval gates.

Choose structure from the document's job:

- Proposal/decision paper: problem, options, criteria, evidence, recommendation, tradeoffs and open decisions.
- Specification: intended behavior, scope, interfaces, constraints and acceptance criteria.
- Procedure: prerequisites, roles, steps, exceptions and completion evidence.

Preserve the user's format, terminology, language and locale. Do not invent policies, approvals, owners, dates or jurisdictional requirements. Distinguish supplied facts, interpretations and proposals. Keep consequential sources close to the claims they support.

Draft the substantive argument first, then write the summary from the resulting content. On revisions, preserve unaffected sections and reconcile definitions, numbers and references across the document.

Check it as a reader with no chat history: can they identify the purpose, understand the terms, find supporting evidence and know the next step? Resolve contradictions and unexplained assumptions; mark unresolved gaps plainly.

Deliver in the requested medium. Use docx/pdf/pptx only when that format is requested; keep a chat draft in chat and use Markdown for an editable text artifact.
